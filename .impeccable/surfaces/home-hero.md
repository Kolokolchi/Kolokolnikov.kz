# Home hero and roaming cat

Mode: Experience. Target: `public/index.html`, `.wild-home` hero and the page companion.

## Accepted direction

The user requested a Dogstudio-style interactive 3D focal object adapted to the existing KK identity and approved one volumetric mark built from the four original logo contours. Later feedback requires the colored portion to stay connected, with only gentle layer movement. Preserve the existing Russian copy, serif/italic display voice, charcoal surface, and blue/lime/orange color roles.

The desktop composition pairs left-aligned editorial copy with the large sculpture on the right. Mobile places the sculpture above the copy. Materials have real thickness, bevels, reflective metal faces and darker sides. Lighting cycles through the three existing palettes on the same object. Pointer response is small and damped; scroll reframing is restrained.

The user also requested one recognizable large-eyed graphite cat with random poses and pawprints. Peeking, lounging and paired head/tail poses may occur at the same element in different orientations. The paired head and tail emerge from opposite edges of that element, clipped behind it. An occasional full-body glide may cross verified empty side whitespace. During scrolling the cat hides before pawprints appear; the short route leads to its exact next head position. Finish the route, fade and remove all prints, then reveal the same planned pose. Keep copy and controls readable.

## Quality bar

- Geometry follows the four paths in `scripts/brand.mjs`; the colored contours retain their connection throughout the cycle.
- The focal object is actual solid 3D geometry, with a readable silhouette and material depth.
- All existing content and controls remain usable on desktop and mobile.
- Cat and visible pawprints never overlap in time. At most three prints form one short trail to the exact future cat destination, with no added edge line. Each print starts its own 750ms fade after 900ms, even during uninterrupted scrolling. After stopping, commit a reachable destination and complete a fresh final approach when the preview has expired or finished. Fade and remove the prints, then reveal the cat at that endpoint. If layout invalidates the destination, plan and show a new approach before revealing. Narrow gaps may use two or one step.
- Random placement samples all safe anchors, sides and poses, using six jittered positions along each edge. Choose the element before its pose so a large card cannot dominate. Weight against the last eight appearances and nearby repeated positions; never restrict choice to a top-six ranking.
- The gliding cat can be caught. Pressing on it lifts it by the scruff and it changes pose to a held cat: panel 3 with its card taken away (original head with whiskers, front and hind paws and tail in their original places; only the chest the card covered is filled with a plain black silhouette and the thin white outline). One cat: the held pose is drawn at the peeking size (same panel-3 crop width) and grows into it from the smaller glide during the lift. It meows once, hangs under the pointer with a light shadow, swings like a pendulum with the hand's sideways speed and stays in the hand while the page scrolls. Below the hand the nearest surface is found (buttons and fields first, then media, filled boxes, then border-only frames within 72px), and a short glowing line marks its top edge. Released there, it hops down onto that edge hind paws first, slips behind it (clipped at the edge), stays out of sight for a moment and then the regular top peek rises over the same edge at the same size; if that peek would leave the screen it simply stays hidden. With nothing below, it slides on from a free side spot or falls out of the window. A press without a move is a meow and the slide continues; Escape, resize, a hidden tab or an opened menu put it down. Scrolling the page under a still hand counts as a move: the mark follows and letting go lands the cat. Put back (Escape, a cancelled touch) it returns to its own spot only while that is still free, otherwise it is released where the hand is. A mouse press meows at once; a touch or pen press meows on release, where browsers allow sound. On touch a vertical swipe that starts on the cat still scrolls the page (`touch-action: pan-y`, the grab is cancelled); a sideways drag carries it. A falling or hopping cat cannot be caught again. Reduced motion skips the swing, hop and slip.
- Clicking the visible cat's head, tail or gliding body plays one of five distinct natural CC0 meows. A shuffled five-recording bag avoids repeats until exhausted and across bag boundaries. Audio starts only on deliberate activation, including keyboard Enter/Space and touch. The five files (~65 KB) are fetched and decoded with Web Audio at idle time, before any tap, so the meow starts with the click instead of after a network round trip; playback skips each recording's quiet lead-in. The audio context (opening the device costs ~0.2 s) is created silently at the visitor's first trusted click, tap or key press anywhere on the page and suspends after a quiet minute; the next meow resumes it inside its own activation. Until they are decoded, or without Web Audio, one audio element is the fallback. One voice replaces the previous sound on rapid taps. Only the clipped cat pixels intercept pointer input; nearby links remain usable. Hidden cats and completed glides are inert/disabled. Page cleanup stops playback, closes the audio context and releases the media element. Recordings and provenance live in `public/assets/cat/audio/`; playback lives in `public/cat-meow.js`.
- Every pose uses the original pixels of the user's six-panel reference (`public/assets/cat/reference.jpg`), isolated by SVG silhouettes in `public/cat-drawings.js`. Preserve its black fur, thin white outline, oval eyes, whiskers and paw details. Panel 3 rotated 180 degrees supplies the upside-down pose. Do not add the sample block or an edge line. Front paws grip the edge. Paired endpoints share one axis and are recomputed from the live anchor's side and fractional position, including during retreat, resizing and reduced-motion scroll. Perspective-rotated cards are excluded from rectangular masking.
- Cat placement uses a safe real element edge. If the current viewport has no safe edge, defer showing it rather than cover content.
- User pause, reduced motion, hidden-tab and offscreen states stop nonessential scene animation. Reduced motion retains a static sculpture and cat.

## Implementation

`src/hero-scene.js` is bundled locally with Three.js by `scripts/build-hero.mjs` into `public/hero-scene.js`. `public/wild.js` owns visibility and motion preference policy. `public/scroll-cat.js` and `scroll-cat.css` own the independent companion. Existing SVG assets supply the non-WebGL fallback.
